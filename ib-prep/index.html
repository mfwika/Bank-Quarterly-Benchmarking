The roadmap: how people actually get in

Timelines shift every year and differ by country. Check your school's career office and bank websites for current dates.

The recruiting funnel

1 · Foundations

Strong grades, a finance or investing club, leadership roles, any finance-adjacent experience (boutique, Big 4 valuation/transaction services, corporate finance, equity research, PE/VC internships).

2 · Networking

Alumni and bankers via cold emails and LinkedIn. Aim for 2–4 calls a week and track every contact. Referrals get you past resume screens.

3 · Applications

Apply early: many programs fill on a rolling basis. One-page resume, tailored cover letter where required.

4 · Screens

Online tests (numerical, logical, situational), recorded video interviews (HireVue-style), phone screens.

5 · Superday

3–6 back-to-back interviews with analysts up to MDs: fit, technicals, deal discussion. Some firms add a case study or modeling test.

6 · Internship → return offer

The summer internship is the main route to a full-time analyst offer. Work hard, be reliable, catch your own mistakes.

Know the landscape

Bank tiers

  • Bulge bracket: global, full-service (e.g., Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, J.P. Morgan, BofA, Citi). Big balance sheets, every product.
  • Elite boutiques: advisory-focused, big M&A deals (e.g., Evercore, Lazard, Centerview, PJT, Moelis, Perella Weinberg).
  • Middle market: smaller deals, often private companies (e.g., Houlihan Lokey, William Blair, Harris Williams, Baird, Lincoln).
  • Regional / local banks & boutiques: strong in their home market. A good way in, and a good way to lateral later.

Groups

  • Coverage (industry): TMT, Healthcare, FIG (banks/insurers), Industrials, Consumer & Retail, Energy, Real Estate, Infrastructure.
  • Product: M&A, ECM (IPOs, follow-ons), DCM (bonds), Leveraged Finance, Restructuring.
  • Hierarchy: Analyst → Associate → Vice President → Director / SVP → Managing Director.

What an analyst actually does

  • Builds and updates models: comps, DCFs, merger models, LBOs, three-statement models.
  • Makes pitch books and marketing materials: teasers, CIMs, management presentations.
  • Runs deal process logistics: buyer lists, data rooms, NDAs, tracking bids, diligence requests.
  • Checks everything. Accuracy and attention to detail matter more than brilliance in year one.

A typical sell-side M&A process

  1. Preparation: pitch and win the mandate, gather information, build the model, write the teaser and CIM, build the buyer list.
  2. Round 1: send teasers → buyers sign NDAs → CIM → non-binding indications of interest (IOIs).
  3. Round 2: shortlist → management presentations, data room access, site visits → binding letters of intent (LOIs) with a markup of the purchase agreement.
  4. Negotiation & signing: pick the winner, negotiate the purchase agreement (SPA / merger agreement), fairness opinion, sign.
  5. Closing: regulatory and shareholder approvals, financing, close.

Networking that works

Cold email template

Subject: [School] student — quick question about [Bank] [Group] Hi [Name], I'm a [year] at [School] studying [major], and I'm interested in [group / investment banking]. I noticed you also [went to X / worked at Y] and would really value 15 minutes of your perspective on your path and the team. Would you have time for a short call in the next two weeks? Happy to work around your schedule. Best, [Name] · [phone] · resume attached

Coffee chat playbook

  • Research them first: LinkedIn, deals their group announced.
  • Open with a 30-second version of your story.
  • Ask about them: how they chose the group, a deal they enjoyed, what makes analysts successful, culture.
  • Don't ask for a job. At the end ask: "Is there anyone else you'd suggest I speak with?"
  • Thank-you note within 24 hours; update them occasionally (e.g., when you apply).
  • Track name, firm, date, notes, and follow-up in a spreadsheet.

Resume rules

  • One page. Education at the top (students), then experience, then leadership/activities, then skills & interests.
  • Every bullet: action verb + what you did + quantified result. "Built a DCF valuing a $250M target, used in the investment committee memo" beats "Helped with valuation".
  • Include GPA if strong; test scores optional by region.
  • Interests line: specific and true (they will ask about it).
  • Anything on the page is fair game in an interview. Be ready to go deep on every line.

Formula cheat sheet

Enterprise value bridge

EV = Equity Value + Debt + Preferred Stock + Noncontrolling Interest − Cash & Equivalents (+ other debt-like items: leases, unfunded pensions)

Diluted shares (treasury stock method)

New shares = ITM options − (options × strike) ÷ share price Diluted = Basic + New shares (+ ITM RSUs, ITM convertibles)

Unlevered free cash flow

UFCF = EBIT × (1 − t) + D&A (and other non-cash) − CapEx − Increase in NWC

Levered free cash flow

LFCF = Net Income + D&A − CapEx − Increase in NWC − Mandatory debt repayment (discount at cost of equity → equity value)

WACC & CAPM

WACC = E/(D+E) × Re + D/(D+E) × Rd × (1 − t) Re = Rf + β × (Equity Risk Premium)

Beta

βu = βL ÷ [1 + (1 − t) × D/E] βL = βu × [1 + (1 − t) × D/E]

Terminal value

Gordon growth: TVn = FCFn × (1 + g) ÷ (WACC − g) Exit multiple: TVn = EBITDAn × exit multiple PV = TVn ÷ (1 + WACC)^n

Accretion / dilution shortcut

Cost of cash = forgone interest × (1 − t) Cost of debt = interest rate × (1 − t) Cost of stock = acquirer E/P (1 ÷ P/E) Yield of target = target E/P at purchase price Accretive if weighted cost < target yield

Returns (LBO)

MOIC = Exit equity ÷ Entry equity IRR ≈ MOIC^(1/years) − 1 2x/3y ≈ 26% 2x/5y ≈ 15% 3x/3y ≈ 44% 3x/5y ≈ 25% Rule of 72: years to double ≈ 72 ÷ rate

Banks (FIG)

NIM = Net interest income ÷ avg earning assets CIR = Operating expenses ÷ operating income LDR = Loans ÷ Deposits ROE = Net income ÷ avg equity P/BV (justified) = (ROE − g) ÷ (COE − g)
